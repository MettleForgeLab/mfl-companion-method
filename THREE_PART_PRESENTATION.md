# A message, a presentation, and a companion

The three-part arrangement gives a recipient several useful ways into the same body of work.

| Part | Its job | A useful design choice |
|---|---|---|
| Personal message | Explain why the sender thought of this person | Keep the connection specific and the invitation open |
| Visual presentation | Let the person encounter the work | Give actual examples, images, writing, and useful links room |
| Companion | Prepare further exploration with the recipient's assistant | Supply substantive context and follow the person's questions |

## Let each part do its work

The message can be short because the presentation carries the first account. The presentation can be concise because a companion offers deeper preparation. The companion can remain broad enough to answer unexpected questions because it is not a script for walking every person through the same pitch.

Creative works retain their own standing. A reader can enjoy a drawing or a passage of writing directly. The companion can then help investigate its construction, teaching, or relationship to another work if that becomes interesting.

## One account, several invitations

MFL's four accepted introductions share Meet MFL edition 0.4:

- **Friends and Family:** a personal look around the tools, stories, art, and practice.
- **Prospective Clients:** useful things to try and an invitation to bring a difficulty or unfinished idea.
- **Educational Institutions:** inspectable methods, learning possibilities, and work connected to a subject and its learners.
- **Financial Backers:** actual production, reusable methods, and reasons someone might value supporting a work or the practice.

These are starting emphases. A friend can ask a technical question; an educator can enjoy a garden; a prospective backer can want to make something. The shared companion follows the live interest.

## How the parts are delivered

In MFL's working arrangement, the personal message sits beside the sending ZIP so Porter can personalize it. The ZIP carries the PDF, companion, and receiving directions. The message may become the email body.

For teaching the arrangement in this repository, the example bundle gathers the message template, accepted PDF, accepted companion, and directions into one downloadable object. This is new example packaging; it does not change the accepted PDF or companion editions.

The actual example preserves **Meet MFL.txt** and **Meet_MFL_A_Look_Around.pdf** because the receiving directions identify those names. A method template can use generic names; a finished presentation should use consistent real ones.

## Where this can lead

A recipient can read, ask, inspect, compare, revisit, share a question with someone else, or contact Porter. The value is that a short introduction can be followed at the recipient's pace and depth.

The arrangement has been built and used in MFL's own practice. Claims about response rates, sales conversion, learning gains, or broad public adoption would require their own evidence. They are not needed to understand how the pieces work together.
