# Preparation that travels with the visitor

A website can contain far more than a visitor has time to read. Its most useful answer may depend on relationships spread across pages, publications, repositories, examples, and the people behind them.

A companion gives the visitor's own chatbot an authored introduction to that environment. The author does some of the work of making the place understandable in advance. The recipient brings the reason for looking.

## What the file carries

The preparation joins four practical contributions:

| Contribution | What it lets a recipient do |
|---|---|
| A substantive account | Begin discussing the subject without first opening every page |
| Meaningful relationships | Understand how works, tools, people, methods, and public places connect |
| Sources and useful routes | Inspect an underlying object, examine a claim, find a download, or return to the right person |
| Guidance for the encounter | Use that knowledge in proportion to the recipient's question and interests |

The file can describe what exists, explain unfamiliar terms, distinguish a finished work from a proposal, and identify which deeper file would answer a particular question. Good preparation is selective: it contains enough substance to begin, together with useful ways to go deeper.

The recipient can read the file directly. The receiving chatbot can use it as supplied context and working guidance. Preparing the conversation this way does not require the publisher to operate the visitor's chatbot.

## A relationship the recipient brings

The chatbot remains the recipient's assistant. The institution contributes its account of itself; the person decides what they want to explore.

A useful encounter may end in appreciation of a garden, a practical discovery, a disagreement, a comparison with another institution, a question for a human, or a decision to return later. The file should give each of those possibilities room.

Authored guidance matters here. Knowing a great deal about a place does not require reciting it all. The preparation can help the assistant follow a particular interest, explain a relationship, offer a relevant source, and stop when the answer is enough.

## Across connected public places

MFL uses distinct companions for its website, GitHub, and LinkedIn. The public website supplies experiences, explanations, tools, and publications. GitHub supplies source objects and technical work. LinkedIn offers another account of Porter's public practice.

A reader may enter through a PDF, follow a garden into the website, and then ask about the code or teaching behind it. The companion helps explain those connections. When another local companion becomes useful, the person can supply it while continuing the same inquiry.

Keep each account attributable as the conversation travels. A website's explanation, a repository's current code, and a visitor's interpretation contribute different things. Preserving those distinctions helps someone compare, challenge, and build on what they encounter.

The method also offers a way to prepare an introduction spanning more than one institution. That extension needs its own sources and clear attribution; MFL's present cross-surface examples establish a starting point, not universal interoperability.

## After the page is closed

A saved companion remains an object the recipient can keep and supply again. It can support a later conversation, a discussion with a colleague, or a question that only becomes clear after the first visit.

Within a conversation, continued use depends on the host's available context and file handling. A companion does not itself create permanent memory, browse the web, update its contents, or install integrations. Its portable contribution is the preparation.

## What MFL has made

The existing work includes public website, GitHub, and LinkedIn companions; a shared Meet MFL account; and four audience presentations paired with that shared companion and personal-message templates. The [three-part arrangement](THREE_PART_PRESENTATION.md) connects a human invitation, an authored visual presentation, and preparation for further inquiry.

The useful design contribution is the cooperation of these pieces: an institution can make its work easier to encounter, while the recipient retains their assistant, questions, timing, and next move.

Porter is designing for an internet in which more people explore with an AI assistant beside them. Broader adoption and increasingly agentic use are future possibilities. This repository documents an implemented practice that people can inspect and adapt now; it makes no historical-first or exclusive-invention claim.
